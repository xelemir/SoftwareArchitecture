# Stakeholders 

| Stakeholder| Description| Main concerns| Potential conflicts |
|-|-|-|-|
| End users | People using MealSwipe for everyday meal planning |  Fast and diverse recommendations, reduced planning effort, ease of use, overview of costs | Ease of use vs. extensive functionality
| Health-conscious users | People with individual nutritional and fitness goals | Fast and diverse recommendations, reduced planning effort, ease of use, overview of costs, accurate nutritional information, personalized health ratings and goal-fitting recommendations | Nutritional goals vs. budget and taste preferences
| Users with dietary restrictions| Users with allergies, intolerances or dietary preferences| Fast and diverse recommendations, reduced planning effort, ease of use, overview of costs, reliable information about ingredients and strict adherence to dietary guidelines in the recommendations | Strict filtering vs. recipe variety
| Budget-conscious users | Users aiming to reduce or control grocery expenses | Fast and diverse recommendations, reduced planning effort, ease of use, overview of costs, affordable meals, reliable cost estimates, budget compliance | Low prices vs. nutritional quality and variety
| Project manager | Person responsible for project planning, coordination and progress monitoring | Meeting deadlines, managing resources, achieving project objectives | Feature scope vs. development effort
| Software developers | Team members responsible for implementing and maintaining MealSwipe | Modularity, maintainability, performance, testability and integration | Complex personalization vs. maintainability
| External service providers | Organizations providing external recipe, nutrition or AI services through APIs or plugins | Compliance with licensing terms, correct use of provided data | Data access restrictions vs. catalog completeness |
| Marketing | Team responsible for promoting MealSwipe and attracting new users | User acquisition, user engagement, brand awareness, customer satisfaction and differentiation from competitors | Attractive feature promises vs. technical feasibility |
| Regulatory authorities | Authorities responsible for enforcing applicable legal requirements | Data protection, transparency and consumer safety | Personalization and data collection vs. regulatory compliance | 
| Nutrition experts | Potential domain experts supporting nutritional evaluation | Reliable nutritional calculations, transparent ratings and responsible recommendations | Scientific accuracy vs. simplified user-facing ratings |
